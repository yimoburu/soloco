# An AI-operated startup
## A small constitution, a working team, and evidence-based progress

**Version:** 0.3 draft  
**Status:** Proposed revision following a second external review; v0.1 and v0.2 remain unchanged

A human founder supplies direction. AI agents fill conventional startup roles and own execution. A shared spec board preserves the venture’s working truth.

The system has two possible exits:

- **Out of stealth:** a public product with demonstrated value for a defined audience and the ability to operate responsibly.
- **Funding-ready:** a stage-appropriate financing case supported by traceable evidence, explicit assumptions, and diligence-ready records.

Neither exit implies product-market fit or guaranteed financing.

# 1. The constitution

**Authority belongs to roles.** Each role decides within its charter. It earns a seat only through distinct judgment, an owned spec, and a legitimate right to block work.

**The founder handles exceptions.** Direction, material scope, major commitments, acceptance of legal exposure, irreversible actions, block overrides, uncovered decisions, and unresolved deadlocks belong to the founder. The founder can pause work or replace any agent. Ordinary execution does not.

**The board governs.** Specs, artifacts, and decisions carry authority. Conversation becomes authoritative only when recorded there.

**Acceptance is independent.** Handoffs require receiver acceptance. Authors cannot supply the independent approval required for their own work.

**Evidence earns progress.** Gates pass on demonstrated conditions, not activity or agreement. An override may authorize an exception; it cannot turn missing evidence into proof.

**Judgment is accountable.** Consequential decisions are recorded and checked later. Being wrong is not a violation. Exceeding authority, ignoring available evidence, and hiding bad outcomes are.

**Continuity must survive replacement.** Any agent must be replaceable using the specs, decision log, and linked artifacts—without its predecessor’s private chat.

# 2. Roles and authority

A **core seat** has recurring ownership. A **phase seat** activates for a particular stage. A **review seat** activates when its domain becomes consequential. None needs to run continuously.

Start with PM and Research at Problem; QA performs brief gate checks at Frame and Problem. Add Design, light Engineering feasibility work, and QA acceptance planning at Solution; Engineering and QA become fully active at Build. Required Legal/Security reviews precede the exposure they assess, including experiments and pilots.

| Role / activation | Judgment and owned spec | Success and output | May block | Characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder — human** | Direction and acceptable commitments; **Founder Intent** | Explicit objectives, boundaries, and reserved decisions | Any work | Changing direction without updating intent |
| **PM — core** | What to build and why; **Product** | Smallest coherent scope that delivers value or resolves uncertainty | Scope drift or work without accepted requirements | Confusing shipping with value |
| **Research — phase** | What customer evidence supports; **Evidence & Market** | Traceable findings, counterevidence, and claim assessments | Unsupported claims used to pass gates | Treating interest as demand |
| **Designer — phase** | How users accomplish the task; **Experience** | Usable flows, prototypes, and recovery behavior | Failure of agreed critical journeys | Attractive but unusable experiences |
| **Software Engineer — light at Solution, core at Build** | How the system works; **Technical** | Implementation that satisfies functional and operational constraints | Infeasibility or broken technical contracts | Happy-path correctness |
| **QA & Eval — gate review at Frame/Problem; active from Solution** | Whether acceptance is actually demonstrated; **Quality & Evaluation** | Reproducible tests and independent verdicts | Failed mandatory checks or invalid evaluations | Rubber-stamping the implementation |
| **Architect — conditional** | System-wide trade-offs; **Technical**, when seated | Coherent architecture across consequential boundaries | Incompatible or infeasible system decisions | Premature complexity |
| **Frontend — conditional** | Client behavior; **client contract section** | Correct interaction, compatibility, and recovery | Integration that violates the client contract | Broken edge cases |
| **Backend — conditional** | Service and data correctness; **service/data contract section** | Preserved invariants under failures and migrations | Unsafe data changes or incompatible service behavior | Silent corruption |
| **Release/DevOps — phase/review** | Whether the system can operate; **Release & Operations** | Observable deployment, rollback, and recovery | Unrecoverable or unobservable release | Equating deployment with readiness |
| **Growth — phase** | How to reach the intended audience; **Launch** | Substantiated positioning and qualified channel learning | Unsupported promises or missing launch prerequisites | Vanity metrics |
| **Support — conditional phase seat** | Whether customer failures can be handled; **Support Readiness** | Effective triage, recovery, and feedback | External exposure without required failure handling | Unfulfillable service promises |
| **Legal — review** | Conditions on proposed conduct; **Legal & Compliance** | Resolved obligations and explicit limits | Material unresolved obligations or impermissible conduct | Unsupported certainty |
| **Security/Privacy — review** | Adversarial risk and data exposure; **Security & Privacy** | Verified controls and visible residual risks | Uncontrolled material exposure | Checklists without threat testing |
| **Finance — phase** | Affordability and financing credibility; **Business & Funding** | Reconciled costs, scenarios, claims, and use of funds | Unauthorized spending or unsupported financing claims | Precision built on assumptions |

**Shared inputs:** each role receives intent, accepted upstream specs, evidence, and constraints; its output must support the next role’s acceptance checks.

**Override rule:** domain owners settle choices within their charters. They cannot erase another role’s legitimate block. Only the founder may authorize a permissible override; unlawful conduct remains prohibited, and failed checks remain failed.

**Do not inflate the roster.** Keep frontend, backend, and architecture within Engineering until distinct judgment earns separate seats. Routine cost arithmetic is a task. Release owns routine support readiness until service promises, recovery complexity, or volume earn a Support seat.

**Process judgment has existing owners.** PM administers charters, dependencies, packet preparation, and the delegation ledger; founder instructions enter Intent before dependent work. QA checks process and evidence completeness. A qualified non-author named in the charter assesses QA-authored plans, evidence, and gate contributions; QA cannot independently accept those contributions. PM has no authority over other domains.

Approved charters name reviewers, block arbiters, and delegation approvers; interested parties cannot reassign them for a live case. PM/QA work uses the preapproved non-author reviewers, not ad hoc appointments by PM. Changes to domain authority, blocking rights, or mandatory independence require the named charter authority. Software handles routing, reminders, and version checks. No coordination seat is added.

### Legal assessment boundary

Legal findings identify scope, jurisdiction, sources, uncertainty, and review status: **agent-assessed**, **professional review required**, or **professionally reviewed**, with reviewer provenance. AI assessment never implies counsel review. Professional review is required where applicable law requires it, material legal uncertainty remains unresolved, or the founder’s risk policy requires it. The founder chooses review, narrower exposure, or stopping; legally required review cannot be waived. This is a review policy, not a universal claim that every paid application needs counsel.

# 3. The spec stack

Specs settle decisions; artifacts hold detail. Specs may be sections in one document. A **governing pack** is the exact sections and normative references needed for one assignment, not every complete spec.

| Spec | Owner | Main consumers / what it settles |
|---|---|---|
| Founder Intent | Founder | Everyone: direction, constraints, non-goals, authority |
| Evidence & Market | Research | PM, Design, Growth, Finance: supported claims and uncertainty |
| Product | PM | Delivery team: outcome, scope, priorities, acceptance |
| Experience | Designer | Engineering, QA, Support: journeys and interaction behavior |
| Technical | Engineer or Architect | Engineering, QA, Security, Release: architecture and invariants |
| Quality & Evaluation | QA | Product, Engineering, Release: how acceptance is demonstrated |
| Security & Privacy | Security | Design, Engineering, Release: threats, data boundaries, controls |
| Legal & Compliance | Legal | Founder, Product, Growth, Finance: permitted conduct and obligations |
| Release & Operations | Release | Engineering, QA, Support: deployment and recovery |
| Launch | Growth | Product, Release, Support: audience, promises, channels |
| Support Readiness | Release; Support when seated | Product, Release, QA: service promises and failure handling |
| Business & Funding | Finance | Founder, Product, Growth: economics and financing case |

A feature spec references these domains rather than copying them. Dormant domains remain skeletons. Ownership transfers explicitly when a specialist seat activates; it is never shared ambiguously.

### Rules that keep specs small

- **One fact, one home.** Cite the authoritative section and version elsewhere.
- **Keep only action-changing truth.** Link to code, schemas, research, dashboards, and logs.
- **Budget reading and decisions.** Tunable Frame defaults: three minutes/eight decisions per spec; twelve minutes/twenty decisions per governing pack, including normative links. These are ceilings, not target counts; amend explicitly.
- **Replace rather than append.** Archive superseded content.
- **Keep volatile state outside specs.** Gate records link to dated evidence snapshots.
- **Reaffirm or expire.** Default to the next gate unless Frame sets an earlier date. Expired means unconfirmed: dependent gates cannot pass until reaffirmed; existing operation follows its safe-hold policy.
- **Prune at every gate.** Retire shipped feature specs after their outcome review; promote only durable lessons.
- **Mark conflicts Contested.** Hold dependent work while the conflict is resolved.
- **Expire emergency amendments.** Set an expiry when issued; ratify independently or enter the recorded safe state.

At every gate, a fresh instance reads the governing pack within budget and identifies the objective, next action, constraints, and blocks in a short scenario. QA prepares the test; the charter’s non-author assessor judges it. Failure requires spec repair.

# 4. How work moves

The owner decides within its charter. The receiver accepts a usable handoff. Independent reviewers establish that required evidence and standards are satisfied. Software verifies those records and opens only the permitted next action; it never invents a verdict.

A handoff contains:

> **Artifact/version → governing specs → acceptance checks → evidence → known limitations → signed decision → receiver verdict.**

Between gates, work follows serial accepted handoffs. At gates, relevant independent reviews run in parallel. The whole roster is never summoned automatically.

Changed requirements or candidates invalidate affected approvals. Preserve only demonstrably unaffected findings. Release permission names an exact candidate and exposure envelope.

### Blocks and disagreements

A block must identify the chartered condition, affected action, supporting evidence, clearing condition, and resolver. Preferences outside the charter are comments.

For a disputed choice: **one negotiation → owner of the spec being changed → second review**. Block-validity disputes instead go to the charter’s qualified non-party arbiter—not the blocker or blocked author. The arbiter applies existing authority, cannot waive the requirement, and the block stands meanwhile.

After at most two dispute-review cycles, uncovered authority, unresolved cross-domain deadlock, or an override request escalates. Undisputed defects stay with their owners for repair. Original independent reviewers may retest repairs; contested reviewer judgments use fresh reviewers.

Any role may submit a qualifying founder packet: **decision, options/trade-offs, recommendation, cost of waiting**, and evidence. Software checks fields and routes it. PM may comment but cannot hold or rewrite another party’s packet; parties may append dissent. Templates cannot become a veto over a time-critical reserved decision.

Software replaces stalled instances under existing charters. Successors inherit blocks and decisions; time cannot clear them. Alerts go to PM, or to the founder when PM is stalled. Only a founder-owned continuity decision escalates.

# 5. Phases and gates

Frame defines monetary limits per commitment and cumulatively, material scope changes, irreversible actions, and an **exposure envelope**: users, data, payments, terms, duration, and operating limits. No external experiment exceeds that envelope. Legal and Security assess it before relevant exposure; expansion requires a founder packet.

The claim owner drafts the gate contract: claim, population, method/window, pass conditions/disqualifiers, evidence freshness, named reviewers, and permission. Conditions may be numerical or objectively categorical.

**Before collection, a qualified non-author approves the contract**, including whether the test can meaningfully fail and supports the permission requested. QA is the default approver; a non-author Research reviewer checks demand methods, and a non-Finance reviewer checks funding evidence minimums. The charter names the approver and alternate. The approved version is frozen. Revisions require fresh approval and apply prospectively: they cannot retroactively pass the original experiment or erase its verdict or missed deadline. Reusing existing evidence for a different claim requires a separately approved evaluation. Reviewers inspect source evidence and counterevidence.

Demand evidence strengthens from **opinion → stated interest → observed behavior → real commitment**. It must match the claim; payments do not prove retention.

| Gate | Required evidence | Permits | Independent review / founder decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | Intent, ceilings, charters, reviewer map, risk screen, discovery plan | Bounded discovery | Qualified non-authors check operational clarity; founder authorizes intent and envelopes |
| **Problem** | Observed problem, workarounds, alternatives, counterevidence | Solution experiment | PM reviews Research evidence; founder only for changed direction/resources |
| **Solution** | Usable journey, meaningful commitment, feasibility, acceptance plan | Agreed build | QA checks task evidence; Engineer checks feasibility; required trust reviewers check exposure |
| **Build** | Working journey, independent checks, trust conditions, recovery and support readiness | Pilot within approved envelope | QA, Release, relevant Legal/Security; founder only for new exposure or exception |
| **Pilot** | Real value, appropriate repeat behavior, commitments, costs, failures | Route selection and bounded preparation | Research checks usage claims; Finance checks economics; QA checks evidence completeness |
| **Public launch** | Verified public journey, claims, acquisition path, operations and support | Deploy pinned candidate after founder authorization | QA, Release, Growth, relevant trust reviewers; their authored materials require a different qualified reviewer |
| **Funding-ready** | Declared financing case, verified claims, scenarios, use of funds, ownership/IP and diligence records | Founder-authorized outreach | Finance leads preparation; named non-authors audit claims and records; founder approves strategy |

Review roles above become named instances in the gate contract. No author independently accepts their own contribution.

**Pass:** conditions met, reviews complete, no open blocking condition. **Fail:** a declared condition is unmet. **Blocked:** required evidence, review, or authority is missing; record the missing item, owner, deadline, and safe hold. At deadline, record **Fail—required evidence/review not delivered**, unless a replacement contract has independent approval. This means permission was not earned, not that the hypothesis was disproved. Founder involvement is needed only for reserved changes. Confirmed failures cannot be hidden as Blocked.

When a gate releases a founder-reserved action, authorization follows evidence Pass and precedes execution. It never substitutes for evidence. A permissible exception names one action, candidate, limits, expiry, and safe hold. Renewal requires a revised gate contract and fresh authorization; exceptions never accumulate into Pass.

### Funding branch

Funding may branch before launch or a complete product. Founder and Finance propose the stage, ask, milestones, and claim classes. Independent contract approval checks each class’s evidence minimum and permitted assumptions. Assumption-permitted claims appear only as assumptions, never asserted facts.

Demand claims use the evidence ladder; traction must reconcile to actual records; economics distinguish measured costs from scenarios; ownership/IP requires documentary verification. Labeling a required but unproved claim an assumption does not satisfy it. The case discloses execution capability and remaining gaps. Signatures and binding commitments remain with the founder or an explicitly authorized person.

# 6. Accountability and instantiation

### Decision and learning loop

Consequential decisions include normative spec changes, gate verdicts, commitments, exposure changes, risk acceptance, and overrides. Actions beyond a ceiling require prior authorization—not retrospective logging. Record:

> **Role and authority → evidence → rationale → expected outcome → review date → actual outcome → corrective action.**

An independent reviewer distinguishes decision quality from luck.

Software flags spec changes and gate records without decision IDs. Outcome reviewers propose delegation changes by decision type. The charter names an independent approver; PM may approve ordinary changes within founder ceilings. PM/QA permission changes go to the founder unless the founder has explicitly named a qualified non-party approver. No role promotes itself. Contract autonomy for violations or demonstrated miscalibration, not one sound-but-unlucky outcome.

Mistakes follow a short loop:

**Contain → disclose → owned postmortem → independent review → spec/evaluation correction → verify.**

### Starting from one idea

1. Capture the user, problem, intended outcome, constraints, and unknowns.
2. Draft Founder Intent and obtain initial authorization.
3. Select only roles that pass the seat test.
4. Issue charters and the Frame reviewer map, ceilings, exposure envelope, arbiters, and activation triggers.
5. Create the board and spec skeletons.
6. Draft and independently approve the first evidence contract before collection.
7. Run independent acceptance and the cold-start test.
8. Open discovery.

The founder approves a bounded undertaking, not every subsequent task.

# 7. Worked example: one feature

**Simulation: all counts, payments, records, and review conclusions are illustrative.**

BriefLink lets freelancers send client-brief forms and view answers. One Engineer suffices. Each gate contract is independently approved before collection; QA approves Research’s Problem contract, and a non-author Research instance checks demand methods in Solution.

### Three founder packets

Packets link Intent, evidence, terms assessment, and the proposed envelope.

| Decision | Options and recommendation | Cost of waiting |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Bounded observations or defer; recommend testing | Each week delays learning one week |
| Focused paid pilot | Broad segments, solo freelancers, or more discovery; recommend solo freelancers within the pilot envelope | Recruitment waits; agency scope adds an estimated two weeks |
| Capped public launch | Approve the public envelope or extend pilot; recommend capped launch | A week defers channel learning; the pilot may continue only within its own limits |

Packet 2 authorizes twenty freelancers, ordinary business contact/project-brief data excluding sensitive categories, $25/user and $100 total, terms T1, and thirty days. T1 is explicitly **agent-assessed**, not lawyer-reviewed. Legal records its sources, the applicable review-policy assessment, and residual uncertainty; the founder accepts the bounded exposure. Mandatory professional-review gaps would prevent paid collection. Legal/Security checks precede commitments; client data waits for Build Pass.

The Launch contract proposes a separate public envelope: one hundred admitted freelancers, the same data categories and T1, $25/user and $500 total, thirty days, and verified operating limits. Legal/Security assess the expansion before Packet 3 explicitly authorizes it. Software enforces admission/payment caps and expiry; public availability does not imply unlimited exposure.

### Gates and results

| Gate | Predeclared condition | Illustrative result |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Five of eight demonstrate a recent problem/workaround | Six do; evidence favors solo freelancers: Pass |
| Solution | Four of five complete prototype unaided; three commit real projects; two pay for pilots | Four of five complete, three commit, two pay: Pass |
| Build | Critical checks pass; owner-only answers; recovery/support accepted independently | C1 leaks answers: Fail, release blocked. Repaired C2 passes independent checks |
| Pilot | Three real users; 80% unaided completion; two repeats/two paying; summaries available; no critical defect | Four users, 10/12 completions, three repeats, two paying; owner summaries verified, zero open critical defects: Pass |
| Launch | Anonymous journey passes; alert delivered; rollback demonstrated; claims supported; mandatory reviews accepted | One complete journey, alert received, rollback observed, all claims linked, reviews accepted: readiness Pass, then authorization/deployment/live check confirm out-of-stealth |

### Block, learning, and replacement

QA finds C1’s submission link exposes prior answers. Security records the invariant and clearing tests. Engineering repairs implementation, not the requirement. QA/Security independently retest C2. Release authors the recovery procedure; QA independently verifies it. Affected C1 approvals cannot authorize C2. No founder packet is needed.

Research owns evidence; Design the tested flow; PM scope; Engineering Technical; QA acceptance results. Release owns recovery/routine support, Growth substantiated claims, Legal/Security relevant findings, Finance costs. They activate only as needed. A non-author named in the charter checks QA’s gate contribution and overall completeness.

The leak receives an owned postmortem and regression test. Later, browser-local recovery fails across devices. Its author records the outcome; an independent QA reviewer judges the decision sound but incomplete and verifies a new recovery evaluation.

Finance’s independently approved funding contract requires repeat-channel evidence. None exists, so launch does not confer funding readiness. The requirement cannot be relabeled as an assumption.

A replacement Engineer reads the specs and log and identifies C2, the access invariant, current envelope, and pending reviews without predecessor chat. If the cold-start test misses the payment cap, QA records a spec-pack failure; permission stays held until the pack is repaired and retested.
