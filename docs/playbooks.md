# Optional playbooks

These examples illustrate the operating loop in the spec. They are not a required company lifecycle or additional governing rules. Adapt evidence and permissions to the founder's objective and exposure; preparation alone grants no execution authority.

## Milestone examples

| Gate | Required evidence | Permission and independent review |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | Intent, ceilings/envelope, review and notification policies, verified delivery path, charters/reviewer map, initial role qualifications, risk screen, discovery plan | Bounded discovery after independent clarity checks and founder authorization |
| **Problem** | Observed problem/workarounds, alternatives, counterevidence | Solution experiment; PM reviews Research evidence |
| **Solution** | Usable journey, meaningful commitment, feasibility, acceptance plan | Agreed build; QA checks task evidence, Engineering feasibility, trust reviewers exposure |
| **Build** | Working journey, independent checks, trust conditions, recovery/support | Pilot within approved envelope; QA, Release, relevant Legal/Security |
| **Pilot** | Real value, appropriate repeat behavior, commitments, costs, failures | Evidence for the next bounded operating or development proposal; Research checks usage, Finance economics, QA completeness. Expansion still requires applicable reviews and authorization |
| **Public launch — optional** | Verified public journey, claims, acquisition path, operations/support | Pinned deployment after founder authorization of the public envelope; QA, Release, Growth, relevant trust reviewers |
| **Funding-ready — optional** | Declared financing case, verified claims, scenarios, use of funds, ownership/IP, diligence records | Founder-authorized outreach; Finance prepares, named non-authors audit |

## Worked example

**Illustration, not observed evidence:** BriefLink helps freelancers collect client briefs. Contracts and thresholds below are company-specific, approved independently before evidence collection. A/R suffixes denote separate, qualified author/reviewer instances; no author supplies their own required approval.

1. **Frame → Problem.** The founder describes the idea in a project chat. Chief of Staff drafts Intent and Operations, PM proposes scope, and independent reviewers check Frame, initial role qualifications, and the notification path. The founder authorizes bounded discovery and a professional-review budget/policy for paid-pilot terms. Research-A finds six of eight recent workarounds against a five-of-eight threshold; PM accepts source evidence and recommends solo freelancers.
2. **Solution.** Legal-A obtains the required professional review of terms T1; Legal-R verifies policy compliance and Security reviews exposure. A founder packet compares the focused pilot, broader agency scope, and more discovery. The founder authorizes twenty freelancers, ordinary contact/brief data excluding sensitive categories, $25/user and $100 total, T1, thirty days. Design supplies a prototype: four of five users finish unaided, three commit projects, and two pay, meeting the predeclared conditions. QA-A checks task results, Research-R checks commitments, and Engineering confirms feasibility: Pass. QA-A writes build acceptance; QA-R reviews it. Client data remains held until Build Pass.
3. **Build.** Engineer submits C1; QA-A finds a submission link exposing prior answers. Security blocks release against the access invariant. Engineer repairs C2; QA-A and Security retest. Release writes recovery/support procedures; QA-A verifies them and QA-R checks QA-A's contribution. C1 approval cannot authorize C2. The team records the incident, adds a regression check, and schedules independent outcome review. Routine repair needs no founder packet.
4. **Pilot → Launch.** Against a contract requiring three users, 80% completion, two repeats/two paying, and no critical defects, Research-A reports four users, 10/12 completions, three repeats, two paying, and zero critical defects. Research-R verifies source records and Growth's claims; Finance-R checks Finance-A's economics. Launch review verifies the public journey, alert delivery, rollback, and mandatory trust reviews. The founder authorizes a separately reviewed public envelope: one hundred freelancers, the same data/T1, $25/user and $500 total, thirty days. Release deploys pinned C2 and verifies the live journey; software enforces the envelope. The founder is not seeking funding, so no financing milestone is required; any later financing case would need its own evidence and authorization.
5. **Operate and improve.** Founder Intent sets an illustrative goal of $1,000 monthly profit after the agreed costs, including AI and founder compensation, no more than five founder hours per week, and continued customer value over six consecutive months, reviewed monthly. The first month meets the profit target but needs eight founder hours weekly: the team reports mixed results, proposes a support improvement within existing limits, and does not claim sustainability. Release maintains the service and Research checks customer value; Finance reconciles costs and cash. Reviews continue after six qualifying months, with operating authority renewed before expiry. Funding remains optional.
6. **Replacement.** A successor must recover C2, the access invariant, exposure limits, open blocks, and due reviews from company records. Missing a cap fails the cold-start check and requires spec repair. Whole-team replacement follows [the continuity contract](../SPEC.md#7-company-continuity-and-team-evolution); the founder does not rebrief each agent.

The founder handles discovery/review-budget, focus/paid-exposure, and public-launch decisions. The team handles domain choices, handoffs, reviews, repairs, and ongoing operations within the authorized envelope.
