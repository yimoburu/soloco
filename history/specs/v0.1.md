# An AI-operated startup
## A small constitution, a working team, and evidence-based progress

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Design baseline for review

A human founder supplies direction. AI agents fill conventional startup roles and own execution. A shared spec board preserves the venture’s working truth.

The system has two possible exits:

- **Out of stealth:** a public product with demonstrated value for a defined audience and the ability to operate responsibly.
- **Funding-ready:** a stage-appropriate financing case supported by traceable evidence, explicit assumptions, and diligence-ready records.

Neither exit implies product-market fit or guaranteed financing.

# 1. The constitution

**Authority belongs to roles.** Each role decides within its charter. It earns a seat only through distinct judgment, an owned spec, and a legitimate right to block work.

**The founder handles exceptions.** Direction, material scope, major commitments, legal exposure, irreversible actions, uncovered decisions, and unresolved deadlocks belong to the founder. Ordinary execution does not.

**The board governs.** Specs, artifacts, and decisions carry authority. Conversation becomes authoritative only when recorded there.

**Acceptance is independent.** Handoffs require receiver acceptance. Authors cannot supply the independent approval required for their own work.

**Evidence earns progress.** Gates pass on demonstrated conditions, not activity or agreement. An override may authorize an exception; it cannot turn missing evidence into proof.

**Judgment is accountable.** Consequential decisions are recorded and checked later. Being wrong is not a violation. Exceeding authority, ignoring available evidence, and hiding bad outcomes are.

**Continuity must survive replacement.** Any agent must be replaceable using the specs, decision log, and linked artifacts—without its predecessor’s private chat.

# 2. Roles and authority

A **core seat** has recurring ownership. A **phase seat** activates for a particular stage. A **review seat** activates when its domain becomes consequential. None needs to run continuously.

Start small: PM and Research for discovery; Design for solution testing; Engineering and independent QA for delivery.

| Role / activation | Judgment and owned spec | Success and output | May block | Characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder — human** | Direction and acceptable commitments; **Founder Intent** | Explicit objectives, boundaries, and reserved decisions | Any work | Changing direction without updating intent |
| **PM — core** | What to build and why; **Product** | Smallest coherent scope that delivers value or resolves uncertainty | Scope drift or work without accepted requirements | Confusing shipping with value |
| **Research — phase** | What customer evidence supports; **Evidence & Market** | Traceable findings, counterevidence, and claim assessments | Unsupported claims used to pass gates | Treating interest as demand |
| **Designer — phase** | How users accomplish the task; **Experience** | Usable flows, prototypes, and recovery behavior | Failure of agreed critical journeys | Attractive but unusable experiences |
| **Software Engineer — core during build** | How the system works; **Technical** | Implementation that satisfies functional and operational constraints | Infeasibility or broken technical contracts | Happy-path correctness |
| **QA & Eval — core from validation** | Whether acceptance is actually demonstrated; **Quality & Evaluation** | Reproducible tests and independent verdicts | Failed mandatory checks or invalid evaluations | Rubber-stamping the implementation |
| **Architect — conditional** | System-wide trade-offs; **Technical**, when seated | Coherent architecture across consequential boundaries | Incompatible or infeasible system decisions | Premature complexity |
| **Frontend — conditional** | Client behavior; **client contract section** | Correct interaction, compatibility, and recovery | Integration that violates the client contract | Broken edge cases |
| **Backend — conditional** | Service and data correctness; **service/data contract section** | Preserved invariants under failures and migrations | Unsafe data changes or incompatible service behavior | Silent corruption |
| **Release/DevOps — phase/review** | Whether the system can operate; **Release & Operations** | Observable deployment, rollback, and recovery | Unrecoverable or unobservable release | Equating deployment with readiness |
| **Growth — phase** | How to reach the intended audience; **Launch** | Substantiated positioning and qualified channel learning | Unsupported promises or missing launch prerequisites | Vanity metrics |
| **Support — phase** | Whether customer failures can be handled; **Support Readiness** | Effective triage, recovery, and feedback | External exposure without required failure handling | Unfulfillable service promises |
| **Legal — review** | Conditions on proposed conduct; **Legal & Compliance** | Resolved obligations and explicit limits | Material unresolved obligations or impermissible conduct | Unsupported certainty |
| **Security/Privacy — review** | Adversarial risk and data exposure; **Security & Privacy** | Verified controls and visible residual risks | Uncontrolled material exposure | Checklists without threat testing |
| **Finance — phase** | Affordability and financing credibility; **Business & Funding** | Reconciled costs, scenarios, claims, and use of funds | Unauthorized spending or unsupported financing claims | Precision built on assumptions |

**Shared inputs:** each role receives founder intent, relevant accepted upstream specs, source evidence, and constraints. Its output is an owned spec or artifact that the next role can accept.

**Override rule:** domain owners settle choices within their charters. They cannot erase another role’s legitimate block. Only the founder may authorize a permissible override; unlawful conduct remains prohibited, and failed checks remain failed.

**Do not inflate the roster.** One Engineer normally handles frontend, backend, and architecture for a simple application. Split those seats only when each needs distinct, recurring judgment and blocking authority. Likewise, routine cost calculations or support replies are tasks; they do not automatically justify Finance or Support seats.

Coordination—routing, reminders, dependency checks, status triggers, and digest assembly—is software. PM owns product priorities, not universal arbitration.

# 3. The spec stack

Specs settle decisions; artifacts hold detail. A spec can be a short section inside a shared document.

| Spec | Owner | Main consumers / what it settles |
|---|---|---|
| Founder Intent | Founder | Everyone: direction, constraints, non-goals, authority |
| Evidence & Market | Research | PM, Design, Growth, Finance: supported claims and uncertainty |
| Product | PM | Delivery team: outcome, scope, priorities, acceptance |
| Experience | Designer | Engineering, QA, Support: journeys and interaction behavior |
| Technical | Engineer or Architect | Engineering, QA, Security, Release: architecture and invariants |
| Quality & Evaluation | QA | Product, Engineering, Release: how acceptance is demonstrated |
| Security & Privacy | Security | Design, Engineering, Release: threats, data boundaries, controls |
| Legal & Compliance | Legal | Founder, Product, Growth, Finance: permitted conduct and obligations |
| Release & Operations | Release | Engineering, QA, Support: deployment and recovery |
| Launch | Growth | Product, Release, Support: audience, promises, channels |
| Support Readiness | Support | Product, Release, QA: service promises and failure handling |
| Business & Funding | Finance | Founder, Product, Growth: economics and financing case |

A temporary feature spec references these domains rather than copying them. Dormant domains remain skeletons until needed.

### Rules that keep specs small

- **One fact, one home.** Cite the authoritative section and version elsewhere.
- **Keep only action-changing truth.** Link to code, schemas, research, dashboards, and logs.
- **Budget reading and decisions.** Each spec and governing pack has an explicit reading-time budget and limit on active decisions. Required linked material counts.
- **Replace rather than append.** Archive superseded content.
- **Keep volatile state outside specs.** Gate records link to dated evidence snapshots.
- **Reaffirm or expire.** Set a review date or phase boundary; material changes trigger earlier review.
- **Prune at every gate.** Retire shipped feature specs after their outcome review; promote only durable lessons.
- **Mark conflicts Contested.** Hold dependent work while the conflict is resolved.
- **Expire emergency amendments.** Ratify them independently or return to the approved safe state.

At every gate, a fresh agent must read the governing pack within budget and correctly identify the objective, next permitted action, constraints, and blocks. If it cannot, repair the specs.

# 4. How work moves

Three acts stay separate:

1. **The owner decides** within its charter.
2. **The receiver accepts** that the artifact supports the next task.
3. **Independent reviewers establish** that required standards and evidence are satisfied.

Software opens the next stage only when the required records exist. It does not invent a verdict.

A handoff contains:

> **Artifact/version → governing specs → acceptance checks → evidence → known limitations → signed decision → receiver verdict.**

Between gates, work follows serial accepted handoffs. At gates, relevant independent reviews run in parallel. The whole roster is never summoned automatically.

A changed requirement makes affected work and approvals stale. Re-review the affected dependencies, not everything indiscriminately. Release approval applies to an exact candidate and exposure envelope.

### Blocks and disagreements

A block must identify the chartered condition, affected action, supporting evidence, clearing condition, and resolver. Preferences outside the charter are comments.

The ladder is:

**One negotiation → domain authority → second review → founder if a genuine block remains.**

No disagreement gets more than two review cycles. Ordinary responsibilities cannot be transferred to the founder merely through persistent disagreement.

A founder packet contains:

> **Decision needed**  
> **Options and trade-offs**  
> **Recommendation**  
> **Cost of waiting**, with evidence links

Work also has deadlines or heartbeats. A disappeared agent creates a visible stalled assignment and safe hold. Silence never means approval.

# 5. Phases and gates

Before collecting results, declare the claim, population, method, observation window, pass condition, disqualifiers, reviewers, and permission gained.

Evidence strengthens from:

**Opinion → stated interest → observed behavior → real commitment of time or money.**

Strength alone is insufficient: evidence must support the particular claim.

| Phase / gate | Required evidence | Founder involvement |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | Accepted intent, charters, resource envelope, risk screen, discovery plan | Authorize direction and boundaries |
| **Problem** | Observed problem behavior, workarounds, alternatives, and counterevidence | Change direction or stop if needed |
| **Solution** | Usable critical journey, meaningful commitment, bounded feasibility | Approve material scope or exposure changes |
| **Build** | Working feature, independent acceptance, required trust checks, pilot readiness | Exceptions or unresolved blocks only |
| **Pilot** | Real use, delivered value, appropriate repeat behavior, commitments, operating costs and failures | Choose a consequential next direction |
| **Public launch** | Working public journey, defensible claims, acquisition path, monitoring, recovery, support | Authorize public exposure |
| **Funding-ready** | Stage-specific case, audited claims, financial scenarios, use of funds, ownership/IP records, risks and diligence index | Approve financing strategy and outreach authority |

Funding may branch before launch—or before a complete product—when appropriate to the financing stage. Missing proof stays labeled.

Gates return **Pass, Fail, or Blocked**. A founder exception authorizes a bounded action while the unmet gate remains unpassed.

# 6. Accountability and instantiation

### Decision and learning loop

For each consequential decision, record:

> **Role and authority → evidence → rationale → expected outcome → review date → actual outcome → corrective action.**

An independent reviewer distinguishes decision quality from luck.

Autonomy expands or contracts **by decision type**, based on comparable reviewed decisions, reasoning, calibration, and disclosure. No role expands its own permissions. Founder-approved ceilings remain fixed unless the founder changes them.

Mistakes follow a short loop:

**Contain → disclose → owned postmortem → independent review → spec/evaluation correction → verify.**

### Starting from one idea

1. Capture the user, problem, intended outcome, constraints, and unknowns.
2. Draft Founder Intent and obtain initial authorization.
3. Select only roles that pass the seat test.
4. Issue charters defining decisions, blocks, limits, and activation triggers.
5. Create the board and spec skeletons.
6. Write the first evidence contract.
7. Run independent acceptance and the cold-start test.
8. Open discovery.

The founder approves a bounded undertaking, not every subsequent task.

# 7. Worked example: one feature

**Illustrative simulation; all numbers below are invented.**

**BriefLink** helps freelancers collect client project briefs. The first feature lets a freelancer send a form, receive answers, and view a summary.

One Engineer handles implementation. Separate Frontend, Backend, and Architect seats are unnecessary.

### Evidence and gates

Conditions are declared before each test.

| Gate | Pass condition | Illustrative result |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Five of eight intended users demonstrate a recent problem and workaround | Six do; solo freelancers show a clearer common need than agencies |
| Solution | Four of five complete the prototype unaided; three commit real projects; two accept a paid pilot | All conditions met |
| Build | Critical journey works; no unauthorized answer access; recovery and support checks pass | First candidate blocked |
| Pilot | Three freelancers use real briefs; at least 80% of attempts complete unaided; two repeat users and two paying users | Four freelancers, 10/12 completions, three repeats, two paying |
| Launch | Public journey, claims, operations, support, and mandatory reviews accepted | Passed after public-exposure authorization |

### A real block

QA discovers that a submission link also reveals prior answers. Security confirms that it violates the existing owner-only access requirement.

Release stops.

The Engineer repairs the implementation. The requirement is not weakened. QA retests independently; Security verifies the access boundary; Release checks the repaired candidate and rollback procedure.

The second review passes. No founder decision is needed.

### Each role leaves an artifact

Research owns the evidence record; Design owns the tested flow; PM owns feature scope; Engineering owns the technical contract and implementation; QA owns acceptance results.

Before external exposure, Release supplies recovery evidence, Support supplies triage procedures, Growth supplies substantiated claims, Legal records required conditions, and Finance checks costs. They activate sequentially or at relevant gates—not all at once.

Finance records unresolved financing assumptions, so public launch does not become a funding-ready claim.

### Only three founder packets

| Decision | Options and recommendation | Cost of waiting |
|---|---|---|
| Authorize discovery | Observe first or build immediately; recommend observation to reduce rework | Delayed learning |
| Narrow to solo freelancers | Serve both segments or focus; recommend the better-supported segment | Definition and recruitment stall |
| Authorize public exposure | Launch or extend the pilot; recommend launch within tested limits | Broader channel learning waits |

### Learning and replacement

Outcome review finds that browser-local recovery fails when users switch devices. Engineering owns the postmortem; Design and Security assess an improvement; QA adds the relevant scenario.

Then the Engineer is replaced. The new instance reads the specs and log, identifies the rejected and accepted candidates, understands the access invariant and pending review, and continues without private chat.

That is the operating standard: enough structure to preserve judgment, evidence, and continuity—without making the founder manage the machinery.
